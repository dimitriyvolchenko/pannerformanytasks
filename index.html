Projects. The row of buttons at the top of Mission control holds your projects, and + starts another one. Every setting - the theme, the bold type, the size of this panel, the look of the notes, the captions, the colour of the strip, the folder Save writes into - belongs to the planner as a whole and applies to every project, the ones here now and the ones made later; only the plan itself lives inside its own project. A new project opens completely empty: press + add a stage under the board, type its name, then add tasks inside it - or open tree and type the whole plan at once. Click a button to switch; each project keeps its own stages, notes, dates, drawing and look, and one press of Save writes them all into a single file. Two small arrows on the open project move it along the row, so the projects stand in the order you want them. A stage can change project too: the crossed-arrows button in the top right corner of a card opens the list of the other projects, and one press carries the whole stage over - its name, its date, its period, every task and subtask with their ticks, their own dates, the i and e marks and the translations on the back. On this board the stage disappears; in the other one it appears at the end. Double-click a project button to rename it (the title above the plan follows), and the small cross on the open one deletes it - press it twice, and the last remaining project is never deleted. Double-click any stage name on the board to rename it in place. The small arrows button in the top right corner of a card turns it over. The other side is the same card - the same number, the same heading, the same date and the same lines in the same order - and every line there can be typed over. Until you touch a line it simply repeats the front, so you translate straight on top of the words. What you write on the back stays on the back: the front is never changed, turning the card to and fro keeps both sides, and a line added on the front appears on the back as a copy of itself.

How it works. Tick tasks to assemble the blueprint - it grows in whole stages, from the ground up, and every new line flies in and settles into place. Row buttons: S adds a subtask, G switches English/Russian, the pencil edits, the number shows the WORK days left for that task - Monday to Friday, weekends are not counted (click it for details), the cross deletes (press ? to confirm). Drag anything by its grip handle (the dotted column): whole stages, single tasks and subtasks - between stages too. Reorder stages with the small arrows next to the number, double-click the number and type the place it should take, or drag a card by its number - the numbering and the calendar on the left follow. At the bottom of every stage: priority lifts that card towards you, and yield in the right corner appears on a lifted card and gives way. The moment one stage gives way the two ranks pull apart: the stage that keeps full priority comes further forward - higher, larger, with a deeper shadow - while the ones that gave way step back, lie almost flat and lose their colour altogether - they turn grey, which on a bright screen is the difference the eye catches first. They stay marked, and colour returns under the pointer. The card in front also takes a little more room above and below itself, so its glow never lands on the card above it. Press yield off, or switch priority off, and everything returns to one level. highlight puts the light on that stage: the whole page behind it - the panel, the drawing, both calendars, the other stages - steps into the shade, and only the lit box stays in colour, lifted towards you. Press it on a second stage and the light holds them both. The two priority lists in Mission control and the counter box beside them carry the same button under them, so a list or the counter can be lit beside a stage. yield light appears on a lit box and gives way inside the light: that box turns grey and a step dimmer than the other lit ones, while the page behind stays dark. Notes take the light too: right-click a sticker (hold it down on a phone) and the menu opens with Highlight this note at the top - the note then stands alone in the light with the page dark behind it, and the same menu offers Yield light and Put the light out. The floating chip that used to say the light was on is gone. In its place, beside the Day button, stands a proper switch: LIGHT. With the light on it reads LIGHT ON, its lamp slides across and turns amber, and one press puts the light out everywhere; press it again and the light comes back exactly on the boxes that had it, so it really is a switch and not a one-way door. Escape still puts the light out too. While the light is on, the panel steps into the shade behind a veil rather than fading away, so the row of tabs - the projects, Day and the switch itself - stays readable and reachable the whole time.
